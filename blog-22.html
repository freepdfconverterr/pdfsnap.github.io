How to Combine Images into One PDF for Free in 2026

Why Combine Images into a PDF

Combining multiple images into a single PDF is one of the most practical document tasks in 2026. Whether you have scanned receipts, photos of a whiteboard, product images, or a series of screenshots — a PDF bundles them into one organized, shareable file that anyone can open on any device.

Sending 10 separate image files looks unprofessional and is harder to manage than one clean PDF. A merged image PDF also lets you control the exact page order, making multi-page scanned documents easy to navigate and submit to portals that only accept PDF uploads.

Common Use Cases

  • Expense reports: Photograph each receipt and combine into one PDF to submit to your employer or accountant.
  • Academic assignments: Photograph handwritten notes or diagrams and submit as a single PDF to an online portal.
  • Property and legal documents: Scan ID cards, passports, and bank statements from your phone and bundle into one submission PDF.
  • Product portfolios: Combine product photos into a single PDF catalog to share with buyers via email.
  • Scanned multi-page forms: Photograph each page of a form and combine in the correct order.

✅ Pro Tip — Name Files in Order First

Before selecting files, name them sequentially — page-01.jpg, page-02.jpg, page-03.jpg. Your OS will sort them in correct order automatically when you select them, saving you the drag-to-reorder step.

Tips for Best Results

  • Keep image resolution consistent. Mixing very high-res and very low-res images in one PDF looks unprofessional. Try to scan all pages at the same DPI.
  • Compress images first for smaller PDFs. Run each image through PDFSnap's Image Compressor before combining — this dramatically reduces the final PDF file size.
  • Check orientation before combining. Rotate any landscape images to portrait (or vice versa) before uploading if you want consistent page orientation throughout.
  • Supported formats: JPG, JPEG, PNG, and WebP are all accepted. GIF images are converted as static frames (animated GIF loses animation).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a limit to how many images I can combine?
PDFSnap has no server-side limit since processing happens in your browser. The practical limit depends on your device's available RAM. Most modern devices handle 50–100 images without issues. For very large batches, combine in groups of 20–30 and then merge the resulting PDFs using PDFSnap's Merge PDF tool.
Will my images lose quality when combined into PDF?
No — PDFSnap embeds your images at their original resolution inside the PDF. No re-compression or quality loss occurs during the PDF creation process. The only exception is if you choose to compress the final PDF afterward.
Can I combine images from different formats (JPG and PNG) in one PDF?
Yes — you can mix JPG, PNG, and WebP images in the same PDF. Each page retains the quality characteristics of its source image.

Step-by-Step Guide to Combining Images into PDF

Here is the exact process to merge multiple images into a single PDF on any device:

On Desktop - Windows and Mac

The easiest method requires no software installation. Open your browser, go to PDFSnap, and drag all your images onto the upload area. You can upload multiple JPG, PNG, or WebP files at once, reorder them by dragging, then click Download PDF.

On Windows, you can also select all images, right-click, choose Print, then select Microsoft Print to PDF as the printer to create a PDF with one image per page.

On Mac, open all images in Preview, select them all in the thumbnail sidebar, then go to File, Print, and Save as PDF.

On iPhone and iPad

Open the Photos app, select the images you want to combine, tap the Share button, scroll down and choose Create PDF. For more control over page size and compression, use PDFSnap directly in Safari.

On Android

Android does not have a built-in image-to-PDF tool. For the cleanest results, use PDFSnap in Chrome - open the site, upload your images, and download the combined PDF.

Controlling Page Size and Layout

When combining images into a PDF, page size matters. Best practices: resize all images to the same width before combining for uniform pages; choose A4 or Letter page size for professional documents; use PNG for screenshots with text (sharper), JPG for photographs (smaller file size).

Reducing File Size After Combining

A PDF made from many high-resolution photos can easily reach 50-100 MB. To reduce this: convert photos to JPG before combining, use 80-90% compression quality, then run the finished PDF through PDFSnap's compress tool. A typical batch of 10 phone photos can be reduced from 40 MB to under 5 MB without visible quality loss.

Extra Frequently Asked Questions

Can I mix JPG and PNG images in one PDF? Yes. PDFSnap accepts mixed formats in the same batch. The output PDF will contain all images regardless of their original format.

Is there a limit to how many images I can combine? PDFSnap handles batches of up to 100 images per session. For larger batches, split into groups and merge the resulting PDFs.

Will my original image quality be preserved? Yes. PDFSnap preserves original resolution by default. If file size is a concern, you can opt for compressed output.

Can I rearrange the image order before creating the PDF? Yes. The drag-and-drop uploader lets you reorder images before downloading the PDF.
